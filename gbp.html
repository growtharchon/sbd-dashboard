The finding

Google local visibility was cut, on the Carlsbad profile only. Carlsbad calls fell 72%, views fell 55% and search impressions fell 84% year over year. San Marcos grew over the same window.

Across both profiles, calls from Google fell 35%, from 417 to 270. That is the phone-side gap. It is not a call-handling problem and it is not explained by the Yelp budget change alone.

The pattern points to Carlsbad being filtered out of non-branded results, most likely as a duplicate of San Marcos. Branded searches still work, which is why it went unnoticed.

Carlsbad's top five terms are 84% branded against 39% for San Marcos, and its search impressions fell 84% year over year. Branded visibility intact, discovery visibility gone, is the specific signature of Google's non-branded visibility filter. It also explains why nobody caught this: searching the business name still returns the profile normally.

Likely cause

Carlsbad is being filtered as a duplicate of San Marcos

Two aggravating factors, both visible in public directory listings.

  • Shared address and phone. Public listings for the Carlsbad-named entity carry 1335 Descanso Ave, San Marcos and (760) 310-1532, the San Marcos location's details. Two profiles for one brand sharing an address and a phone number is the standard trigger for duplicate filtering.
  • Business name outside guidelines. The profile name is "Smith Bros Mobile Detailing, Ceramic, Tint, & PPF". Google requires the real-world name only; appended service keywords can suppress a profile on their own.
  • The mirror image. San Marcos rose 31% across the identical window in which Carlsbad fell 58%. That reads as one profile absorbing the other's visibility rather than as a market-wide change.

Checks to confirm

in order
  1. Incognito, location set to Carlsbad, search "car detailing carlsbad" and "mobile detailing near me". Does the Carlsbad profile appear in the map pack at all, or only San Marcos?
  2. Check the Carlsbad profile for a suspension notice, a pending edit, or a Google-applied name change.
  3. Compare address, phone and primary category across all three profiles. An identical phone or address on two of them confirms the trigger.
  4. Pull Carlsbad month by month. The period picker accepts a single month and returns the same month last year, so five selections date the drop and settle whether April is when it happened.
  5. Pull profile 3 on the same basis. It is not in any number on this page.
  6. Pull Fieldd new customers by source, monthly, April–August both years, to apportion the decline between the Google loss and the Yelp budget change.

Method and limits

  • Why 2025 is derived. The Google interface will not display a date range older than six months, so April–August 2025 cannot be viewed directly. Every card does print its own year-over-year percentage, and the 2025 figures here are back-calculated from those. The result self-validates: on both profiles and in both years the component metrics sum exactly to the interactions total.
  • Calls undercount. Google counts taps on the call button. It does not count anyone who read the number and dialled it, and Google removed call history in July 2024, so there is no answered, missed or converted data available in the platform at all.
  • Three metrics no longer exist. The branded and discovery split was deleted by Google in February 2023; the search-terms table above is the closest available proxy. Standalone profile views were replaced by the views and surface breakdown. Messaging was shut down on 31 July 2024, so any year-over-year comparison there measures Google's shutdown rather than marketing performance.
  • Bookings are zero on both profiles in both years. Reserve with Google is not configured.
  • Search impressions. The searches metric is built from search terms that Google filters for low volume before reporting. On San Marcos, where views are flat and every action rose, its 42% fall is most likely a counting artefact. On Carlsbad it is corroborated by views, calls and clicks all falling together, so there it is real.
