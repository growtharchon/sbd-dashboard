Thin line is each day as billed; the heavier line is the trailing 7-day average, which is what the trend actually looks like.

Over the 90 days SBD billed , an average of a day. Cut into three equal months the totals are $181,384, then $185,443, then $180,257. That is a spread of under 3% across a quarter, so this is a stable base rather than a business trending in either direction.

The visible spikes are single large jobs, not shifts in demand. 18 August billed $17,071, the biggest day in the record and roughly 2.8× a normal day. The most recent full week, 17 to 23 August, came in at $46,691, the strongest week of the 90 days and up 12% on the week before it. August closed the 23rd at $139,501, pacing to roughly $188k against July's $186,805.

The conversion gap, and what it did to August

Now fixed

This is the one chart that needs reading with the tracking fix in mind.

Conversions recorded per day

Conversions Tracking gap

Daily conversions as recorded in Google Ads. The shaded band is 13 to 23 August, when the tracking was broken.

Split August at the 13th and the break is unmistakable. 1 to 12 August: 359 clicks and 25 conversions, a 6.96% conversion rate, the best stretch in the whole 90 days. 13 to 23 August: 372 clicks and 4 conversions, a rate of 1.08%. Eight of those eleven days recorded nothing at all.

Clicks held steady and spend actually rose, so traffic never went anywhere. Revenue over that same stretch was the strongest of the quarter, and the record $17,071 day on 18 August is a day Google Ads logged zero conversions. Jobs were being booked and billed the entire time; only the recording stopped.

The practical effect is that August's cost per lead reads $149.07 and the 13–23 August window reads $605.60. Neither is a real number. July's $88.33 and the $76.02 from the first twelve days of August are the honest recent picture, and both are better than where the account started the quarter.

What to take from it

Working
  • Revenue is dependable. Three months inside a 3% spread, and the most recent week was the best of the quarter.
  • Cost per lead genuinely improved. $132.22 in the first block to $90.51 in the second, a 32% gain before tracking muddied the third.
  • Brand is the efficient campaign at roughly $13 a lead over the last 30 days, against $134 for Performance Max and $243 for Search.
  • Two old flags are closed. Auto-Apply is off, and phone calls now carry a value instead of counting as $0.
Watching
  • August's ad metrics are unreliable until the fixed tracking has run a clean fortnight. Do not read the CPL spike as performance.
  • Search 2026 is the expensive campaign at $243 a lead on $2,675 of spend, the largest single line in the account.
  • Spend doubled on 20 and 21 August. Confirm that was intentional.
  • Desktop still costs more than mobile, $152.29 against $119.92 per lead.
